# Diagnosis → Strategy → Tactics

## When to use this

Reach for this first, at the start of any engagement — it's the top-level lens for deciding whether the open question is a **Diagnosis** question (evidence-gathering), a **Strategy** question (who to target, what position, what objective), or a **Tactics** question (product/pricing/comms/distribution choices), and for keeping later work honest about which phase it belongs to.

## Purpose

This framework sequences marketing work into three connected phases:
diagnosis, strategy, and tactics. Diagnosis establishes what is happening in
the market; strategy makes choices about where and how to compete; tactics
express those choices in market-facing action.

## The three phases

### Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses research and market understanding to describe the situation. It
does not supply a ready-made strategy. The output is an informed view of the
market, customers, competitors, and relevant opportunities or problems.

### Strategy

Strategy converts diagnosis into choices. Its central questions are who to
target, what position to establish, and what objectives to pursue. The strategic
choices determine which opportunities receive attention and which do not.

### Tactics

Tactics implement the strategy through the Four Ps: product, pricing,
marketing communications, and distribution. Tactics are not a substitute for
strategic choice; they are the means by which the market experiences it.

## Operating sequence

1. Gather and interpret evidence about the market.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — output must be real gathered evidence, never invented, executed via a dedicated research-method Playbook.
   **Invokes:** [Market Research Method Sequence](../playbooks/market-research-method-sequence-secondary-qualitative-quantitative-mid-stage-focus-groups.md) (Playbook).
2. Formulate the target, position, and objectives from that diagnosis.
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement, executed via dedicated Playbooks.
   **Invokes:** [Segmentation Process](../playbooks/segmentation-process-top-level-5-step-sequence.md), [Module 4 Targeting Sequence](../playbooks/module-4-targeting-sequence-strategic-framing-philosophy-choice-apply-criteria-build-portrait-ensure-clarity.md) (Playbooks); [Target, Position, Objective](target-position-objective.md) (Framework).
3. Translate those choices into aligned product, pricing, communications, and
   distribution decisions.
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided), executed via the Four Ps.
   **Invokes:** [Four Ps](four-ps.md) (Framework).
4. Review execution against the strategic choices and intended objectives.
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement.

Diagnosis feeds strategy, and strategy drives tactics. The sequence is
repeated explicitly when moving from strategy into tactical work.

## Boundaries

- Do not infer strategy directly from a single research result.
- Do not begin tactical selection before the strategic choices are clear.
- Do not treat tactics as independent creative or commercial ideas.
- Do not treat the sequence as a formal dependency relationship in the
  knowledge model; it is an ordered working logic.
