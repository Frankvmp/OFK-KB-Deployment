# Long-term brand building and short-term activation

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this whenever a communications plan or budget is being built — to check that both future-demand and immediate-response work are represented and coordinated, rather than one crowding out the other or the two being merged into a single execution that does neither well.

## Purpose

This framework separates two complementary forms of marketing activity. Long-
term brand building creates memory structures and future demand. Short-term
activation produces an immediate response and harvests existing demand.

## The two roles

### Long-term brand building

Long-term activity changes how people remember and perceive the brand. It is
usually broad, may operate for weeks or months, and is intended to influence
future behaviour rather than drive an immediate product purchase.

### Short-term activation

Short-term activity evokes an immediate response. It is often targeted to
people likely to respond now and can be evaluated through spend and resulting
sales uplift.

The distinction is about the intended effect and use of the activity, not a
permanent classification of a media channel. The same medium can be used for
long-term brand building or short-term activation depending on the execution.

## How this executes

This framework does not itself state a specific numbered procedure for building a plan — an earlier draft of this section presented a 6-step "Operating model" in that shape, but no footnote supports that specific breakdown. What's actually given, with real citations, is a set of concrete embedded Playbooks below — Zero-Based Budgeting, Triple Cooked Budgeting, the Media S-Curve, the Effectiveness Measurement Escalation Ladder, and the Campaignability sequence — each covering one piece of the long/short planning-and-measurement problem.

Long and short activity should work together. The long creates the opportunity;
the short converts it. Separating their roles does not mean operating them as
unconnected programmes.

## Budget balance

The often-cited 60/40 split is not a fixed rule. It originated in a particular
body of consumer-goods evidence and the correct balance varies with the company,
category, situation, and other strategic factors.

## Supporting Case Study: McDonald’s

A broad McDonald’s campaign is used to illustrate long-term brand building. It
is aimed broadly, refreshes memory structures, builds awareness, and changes
brand perceptions. It is not primarily an immediate product-sales activation.

## Supporting Metrics

No universal allocation metric is attached. The 60/40 figure is retained as
historical contextual evidence, not as a formula or target.

Long-term activity should be assessed through changes in brand outcomes such as
awareness, consideration, or brand perceptions. Short-term activity should be
assessed through response and return-on-investment measures.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not treat long-term brand building and short-term activation as mutually
  exclusive business choices.
- Do not classify a medium as permanently long or short.
- Do not use the 60/40 split as a universal budget rule.
- Do not evaluate long-term brand building only through immediate sales.
- Do not evaluate short-term activation only through long-term brand movement.
- Do not combine both purposes into one execution without recognising the
  different objectives and measures.

### IMC Plumber Diagnostic Sequence

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** None stated.

**Steps:**

1. **Diagnose before touching any tool.** The first thing he did was talk to me. When did the problem begin?... Then he went and had a look at the dishwasher itself.
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement — diagnosing the real problem before choosing channels.
2. **Select the right tools, don't use them all.** he opened up this giant tool bag and before me were at least 20 different tools. He didn't use them all though. He used three or four in conjunction.
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — select a few channels that fit the diagnosis, not every available channel.
3. **Execute.** Fixed the problem in about two or three minutes and before you know it, he was gone.
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement — a real-world execution act the agent can recommend, not perform.

**Output:** Effective, cohesive channel use -- offered as 'a better definition of integrated marketing communications than the one we got from the AMA.'

### Zero-Based Budgeting Process

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** None stated.

**Steps:**

1. **Develop SMART objectives.** I'm going to develop those smart objectives...
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — reuses the real objectives already set, never invented here.
   **Invokes:** [Stated Objective Structure](../schemas/stated-objective-structure.md) (Schema).
2. **Calculate incremental value.** I'm going to calculate their incremental value. How much money will they make if I make them come true?
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — a real financial estimate, never invented.
3. **Brief the agency for cost estimates.** *(Adapted for agent execution — states the evidence standard, not a transcript claim.)* The agent's role is to identify and clearly specify which objectives need a real cost estimate, and how each will be measured and can be improved — preparing a well-defined brief. The actual estimate must come back from a real agency or supplier; the agent cannot invent or predict that figure. If no such estimate exists yet, that gap must be stated honestly rather than filled with an assumed cost.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — the actual cost estimate must be real and externally sourced, never invented; the agent's own task is limited to specifying the ask.
4. **Present investment + return together before budgets are set.** I'm going to bundle that all together, and I'm going to present it in my marketing plan to senior management, crucially, before budgets are set.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — investment and return must be presented together, and before budgets are set, not after.
5. **Branch on approval.** The senior management team can say yes... or... say no... I have to go back to my office and revise my proposed return based on how much money they'll actually give me.
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement — a real approval decision the agent cannot make, only respond to.

**Output:** "you do ensure that communications is an investment and it's linked to the return that it eventually generates." (00:16:44)

**Boundary:** Do not add steps, thresholds, or prerequisites that the source does not specify.

### Triple Cooked Budgeting

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** Offered explicitly as a simpler alternative to ESOV.

**Steps:**

1. **Stage one: size the budget (~10% of revenue) -- 'Kite Boil'.** named after Grace Kite, the Kite Boyle... you want to spend about 10% of your revenues on comms if you want to get the maximal return.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — ~10% of revenue is the stated default, an override rather than a free choice.
2. **Stage two: split into long/short buckets -- Binet & Field.** let's take that 10% and let's split it into long and short buckets... So 6% of the 10% goes into long and 4% goes into short-term.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — the 60/40 split is a stated default, not a universal rule (see Budget balance above).
3. **Stage three: measure each bucket differently -- 'Deep Fat Fryer'.** For the 6%... use brand tracking... for the short investments, absolutely use ROI metrics.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — each bucket must use its own matching measure, not one measure for both.
   **Invokes:** [Brand tracking](brand-tracking.md) (Framework).

**Output:** "In three stages, you've set your budget, you've allocated properly and you've measured the results without any really complicated thinking." (00:26:52)

**Boundary:** Do not add steps, thresholds, or prerequisites that the source does not specify.

### Media S-Curve Apex-Hopping Sequence

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** None stated.

**Steps:**

1. **Find the apex (minimum spend for maximal output).** look for the middle, the apex. The place where the minimum of investment will give us the maximal possible output.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — the real diminishing-returns curve for the channel, never invented.
2. **Stop investing further in that channel once the apex is reached.** Once you've reached that maximal point, put that tool down and move your money to the next tool with the next S.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — stop at the apex; further spend in that channel is against the rule.
3. **Find the next channel's optimum.** And again, find the optimum place.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering.
4. **Repeat across channels.** there's no one good medium, that at some point, once you've invested the right amount, you need to look for others.
   **Step type:** none of the four defined types fits cleanly — this is a loop-back instruction, not a gathering, decision, or judgement act. Flagged rather than force-labelled, per the same gap noted in `market-orientation.md`.

**Output:** Hands off within-module to channel/media mix decisions (00:40:59).

**Boundary:** Do not add steps, thresholds, or prerequisites that the source does not specify.

### Effectiveness Measurement Escalation Ladder

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** None stated.

**Steps:**

1. **Check against stated SMART objectives.** we could simply measure our campaign on whether it achieved or didn't achieve our stated objectives.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — reuses the real stated objective, never invented here.
   **Invokes:** [Stated Objective Structure](../schemas/stated-objective-structure.md) (Schema).
2. **First-/last-touch attribution.** many companies do either first- or last-touch attribution.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — real attribution data, never invented.
3. **Brand lift studies (fixes single-channel underestimation).** *(Adapted for agent execution — states the evidence standard, not a transcript claim.)* Where the campaign spans more than one channel, first-/last-touch attribution's undercount must be checked against a real, controlled comparison of outcomes between people who were genuinely exposed to the activity and comparable people who were not. If no such comparison exists yet, that gap must be stated plainly rather than filled with an assumed uplift.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — real controlled-comparison evidence, never invented.
4. **Media mix modeling (fixes single-channel limit).** There's only one problem. It only measures a single channel... To do that, we have to move to media mix modeling.
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — real media-mix-modeling output, never invented.

**Output:** "at the end of the actual extended period of execution, we learn, we evolve, and we get better." (01:32:03)

### Campaignability / No-Wearout Budget Reallocation Sequence

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** Depends on accepting the 'wear-out is a myth' finding.

**Steps:**

1. **Accept wear-out is not generally evidenced.** the trick here is to learn patience. Resist the pull of new campaigns, remembering that wear out really isn't a thing.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — resist replacing a working campaign on an unevidenced wear-out assumption.
2. **Reuse proven old ads.** dust down your old ads. Some of the ancient ads in your library are probably just as effective now as they were 5, 10 or 15 years ago.
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — the recommended default once wear-out is rejected.
3. **Front-load creative investment in year one.** invest a lot of money in your creative in year one because you know that that ad can run for two, three, four, 10 years.
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — a stated budget-timing rule.
4. **Reallocate later years' budget to media, not new creative.** for years 2, 3, 4... all of your money can be working capital invested into media because you don't need to spend 20 or 30 percent of your budget on any new creative.
   **Step type:** Constraint check — later-year budget must go to media, not new creative.
5. **Plan for campaignability (a big idea that lasts).** plan for what I call campaign ability. Look for a strategy. Look for a big idea.
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement — identifying a big idea capable of lasting is a creative judgement call, not a lookup.

**Output:** A multi-year budget allocation strategy; validated by the Cadbury generosity campaign case study.

**Boundary:** Do not add steps, thresholds, or prerequisites that the source does not specify.
