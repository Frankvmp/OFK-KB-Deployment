# Strategy directs each tactical P

## When to use this

*(Process framing — not a sourced claim; no footnote.)* Reach for this whenever a tactical choice (which campaign, which distributor, which flavour) is being made and the temptation is to judge it on its own creative or commercial merits — use it to force the question back to target, position, and objective instead.

## Purpose

Strategic choices should determine tactical choices. The target, position, and
objective provide the test for selecting product, pricing, marketing
communications, and distribution decisions. [^tactical-choice]

## How this executes

This framework does not itself state a specific numbered decision procedure — an earlier draft of this section presented a 6-step "Decision model" in that shape, but no footnote supports that specific breakdown (see Citation status below). What's actually given is a mantra — target, position, objective — to repeat before any tactical choice, plus three reasons for the discipline: strategy already has the tactical answer, the tactical space is distracting, and the consumer only ever experiences tactics, never the strategy document itself.

**Step type:** Constraint check — every tactical choice must be tested against target, position, and objective before being made.

The consumer experiences strategy indirectly through the combined tactical
choices. Alignment across the Ps creates a coherent brand experience.
[^tactical-choice]

## Application to the four Ps

- Product must serve the target and deliver the intended position.
- Pricing must support the value, position, and objective.
- Marketing communications must express the position and support the objective.
- Distribution must make the offering available in a way that fits the target,
  position, and objective. [^four-ps] [^distribution]

## Supporting Principle: strategy before tactics

Tactical activity without an established strategy is likely to lose direction.
The strategic choices should be settled before tactical alternatives are
selected. [^strategy-origin]

## Supporting Principle: strategy is choice and sacrifice

Strategy concentrates resources by choosing what to pursue and what not to
pursue. A tactical list that attempts to satisfy every possible audience or
objective is not evidence of a stronger strategy.

## Supporting Case Study: Apple focus

Apple is used as an example of strategic focus through explicit rejection of
attractive alternatives. The case illustrates that saying no protects the
chosen strategic priority. [^strategy-origin]

## Metrics

No direct supporting Metric is attached. The framework is a decision test, not a
formula. Tactical performance measures should be selected from the objective
and the specific P being evaluated.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not select tactics because they are fashionable, creative, or convenient
  before checking strategic fit.
- Do not allow a vendor, channel, or internal function to define the strategy
  through its preferred tactic.
- Do not treat distribution as an operational exception to strategic direction.
- Do not confuse tactical agility with changing the strategy whenever execution
  becomes difficult.
- Do not invent a numeric score for strategic fit unless the relevant framework
  supplies one.

### Channel-Conflict Response Sequence (horizontal -> vertical)

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** Explicit: fires only once a large retailer has already broken RRP.

**Steps:**

1. **Trigger: a large retailer breaks RRP, causing horizontal conflict.** there's a tendency for those larger big box retailers to ignore the recommended retail price... horizontal channel conflict. [^channel-conflict-response-sequence-horizontal-vertical-1]
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — a real observed pricing event, never invented.
2. **Escalation: smaller retailers complain, creating vertical conflict.** the smaller retailers complain, the larger retailers are talked to by Gillette, and that creates a different kind of channel conflict... vertical channel conflict. [^channel-conflict-response-sequence-horizontal-vertical-2]
   **Step type:** Data-gathering — real complaint data, never invented.
3. **Response: offer a differentiated value pack.** offer the retailer a different value pack. In this case, a box of 11 razors rather than four. [^channel-conflict-response-sequence-horizontal-vertical-3]
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — the stated resolution once the conflict is confirmed.
4. **Validation: harder for consumers to price-compare directly.** these products and their differences make it difficult for the consumer to compare directly, and therefore it protects... the smaller retail. [^channel-conflict-response-sequence-horizontal-vertical-4]
   **Step type:** Evidence-and-judgement — confirming the response actually achieved its intended effect.
5. **Caveat: creates a new SKU-proliferation problem.** note a problem that we talked about back in Module 6. We've now created a new SKU. [^channel-conflict-response-sequence-horizontal-vertical-5]
   **Step type:** Constraint check — flags a new problem this solution creates, to be weighed against product rationalisation.
   **Invokes:** [Product Rationalisation Path](four-ps.md#product-rationalisation-path-temporary-revenue-reduction-to-focus) (embedded Playbook).

**Output:** A differentiated SKU that resolves horizontal price conflict but seeds a new SKU-proliferation problem.

### Dom Perignon's Two-Step Response to Uncontrollable Discounting

This ordered process is embedded here because it is self-contained within the originating Framework.

**Inputs and preconditions:** Explicit: 'there isn't one [right answer]... there's no kung fu move that's going to give you power over these retailers' -- direct/legal control isn't an option, forcing this workaround.

**Steps:**

1. **Make the product harder to appear discounted/tatty.** delivering the product in such a way that it was much harder to make it appear ripped and rather tatty. [^dom-perignon-s-two-step-response-to-uncontrollable-discounting-1]
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — a real-world packaging/delivery choice the agent can recommend, not perform.
2. **Reinvest revenue/profit into brand advertising.** they went back to exactly what we talked about earlier in this module... there are three gifts we get from distribution. There's brand, there's profit and there's revenue... they decided once and for all to start advertising and advertising far more heavily. [^dom-perignon-s-two-step-response-to-uncontrollable-discounting-2]
   **Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — reallocating real revenue/profit toward brand advertising.

**Output:** Offsets brand-perception damage via reinvested revenue/profit, while keeping the (uncontrollable) Costco revenue and profit gift.
