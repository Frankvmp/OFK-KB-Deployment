# Positioning Statement Build Order (to -> what -> versus -> is)

## When to use this

*(Process framing — not a sourced claim; no footnote.)* Reach for this once a target segment and its portrait exist and the Three Cs test has identified what to position on — it's the build order that turns that analysis into a filled-in positioning statement.

## Purpose

This playbook defines an ordered process for positioning statement build order (to -> what -> versus -> is). The sequence and decision points are retained from the registered evidence.

## Invokes

- [Positioning works backwards from intended customer associations](../principles/positioning-works-backwards-from-intended-customer-associations.md) (Principle) — keeps the completed statement focused on the intended customer meaning.
- [Choose positioning associations by fit, not hierarchy](../principles/choose-positioning-associations-by-fit-not-hierarchy.md) (Principle) — governs the choice of material for the “is” field.
- [Positioning is internal strategy expressed through touchpoints](../principles/positioning-is-internal-strategy-expressed-through-touchpoints.md) (Principle) — prevents the statement from being mistaken for customer-facing copy.

## Ordered steps

### 1. "To" -- who we are positioning to

**Step type:** Data-gathering — reuses the real target portrait already built; not re-derived or invented here.

This comes back from module four. This is the name of the target segment... And remember those portraits that we built last week. [^origin]

**Invokes:** [Target Customer Portrait](../schemas/target-customer-portrait.md) (Schema, Module 4).

### 2. "What" -- the thing being positioned

**Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — a strategic choice among what's actually available to promote (company, brand, product, or a different way of thinking).

The what here is not the benefit... it's the thing I want to position... Sometimes it's the company. Sometimes it's a brand or a specific product. [^step-2]

### 3. "Versus" -- the alternative(s)

**Step type:** Data-gathering — the real alternative(s) the target customer is actually considering, per their portrait, never invented.

the alternative or alternatives that this particular customer is considering for this particular decision... Sometimes it's the status quo. [^step-3]

### 4. "Is" -- the actual positioning claim

**Step type:** Decision (criteria-guided) — choosing among the available positioning-claim types (category entry point, feature, benefit, association).

what am I trying to say... Is it a category entry point? Is it a particular feature? Is it a benefit? Is it some form of association? [^step-4]

**Invokes:** Benefit Ladder (Framework, vocabulary menu). *(not yet drafted)*
