# Channel management depends on interdependent partners

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a distribution decision is being treated as a one-company optimisation despite its effects on wholesalers, retailers, affiliates, or other channel partners.

## Principle

An indirect distribution channel is a system of firms that collaborate to make an offering available for end use. Conflict is inherent, but it is more likely to remain contained when the parties recognise that they depend on one another.

Channel management therefore requires ongoing attention to partner roles, economics, and satisfaction. The aim is not perfect harmony; it is a sufficiently workable system that preserves availability and the contribution of each participant.

## Why it matters

A locally optimal price, pack, or route can weaken another participant and create horizontal or vertical conflict. Unmanaged conflict can reduce cooperation, availability, or channel discipline.

## Related distinctions

Interdependence does not mean every party has equal power or must receive equal margin. It means one firm's choices cannot be assessed as though the rest of the channel were unaffected.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not optimise only the manufacturer's margin.
- Do not assume conflict proves that the channel has failed.
- Do not promise complete satisfaction across all partners.
- Do not use relationship management to justify unlawful influence over a reseller's independent pricing.
