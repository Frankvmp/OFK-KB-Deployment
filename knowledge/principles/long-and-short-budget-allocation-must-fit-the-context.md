# Long-and-short budget allocation must fit the context

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a communications budget adopts a 60/40 brand-building and activation split as a universal default.

## Principle

The 60% long-term and 40% short-term split is an evidence-derived reference point, not a fixed rule. The appropriate allocation varies with the company, category, situation, and other strategic factors. [^balance]

## Why it matters

A historical average can provide orientation, but applying it mechanically can misallocate resources when the brand's conditions differ from the evidence base.

## Related distinctions

Rejecting a universal split does not remove the need to fund both roles. It changes the allocation decision from rule-following to context-sensitive judgement.

## Invokes

- [Long-term brand building and short-term activation](../frameworks/long-term-brand-building-and-short-term-activation.md) (Framework) — provides the two budget roles whose balance must be chosen.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not present 60/40 as a universal optimum.
- Do not choose a custom split without examining the relevant strategic context.
- Do not use contextual variation as a reason to omit one role without justification.
- Keep the source and age of any benchmark visible.
