# Strategy requires choice and sacrifice

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a proposed strategy contains too many priorities or avoids stating what will not receive resources.

## Principle

Strategy concentrates energy and resources on one or a small number of pivotal objectives. Spreading effort across too many priorities weakens focus and reduces the chance of achieving the intended cascade of results.

Choice therefore requires sacrifice. A strategy must state both what the organisation will pursue and what it will deliberately decline, even when the rejected option is attractive.

## Why it matters

Resources are finite. Without explicit non-choices, every activity can remain a priority and the strategy stops directing attention.

## Related distinctions

A list of desirable outcomes is not yet a strategy. Strategy creates a hierarchy by committing resources to selected outcomes and withholding them from others.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not call every opportunity a priority.
- Make rejected options explicit enough to guide resource allocation.
- Do not confuse sacrifice with permanently rejecting an opportunity; strategic choices can have a defined horizon.
