# Omnichannel quality depends on integration, not channel count

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a retailer or brand equates omnichannel maturity with adding more channels, despite fragmented data or an inconsistent customer experience.

## Principle

Operating several channels is not itself omnichannel. Omnichannel maturity requires those channels to work as a consistent, seamless experience in which customer data and the brand experience can move across touchpoints. [^integration]

Because covering every possible channel can be overwhelming, the module advises concentrating on the two or three main channels and integrating them to a high standard. [^priority]

## Why it matters

Adding channels without integrating them can leave the organisation with an unintegrated multichannel system and make the customer experience harder to manage.

## Related distinctions

Multichannel means that several channels exist. Omnichannel requires integration across the priority channels. The cited number is contextual advice from the module, not a universal numerical law.

## Invokes

- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — keeps the distribution experience coordinated with the rest of the market-facing offer.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not count active channels as proof of integration.
- Do not attempt every possible channel before the priority channels work together.
- Do not claim that every organisation must use exactly two or three channels.
- Verify integration through actual customer journeys, data continuity, returns, payment, loyalty, and service capabilities.
