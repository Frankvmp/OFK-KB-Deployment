# Complex thinking should produce simple strategy

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when strategy is either presented as an unexplained slogan or left as a complex analysis that teams cannot apply.

## Principle

The output of strategy should be simple enough for the people responsible for execution to understand and act upon. That simplicity is the result of substantial analysis and judgement, not the absence of them.

## Why it matters

Complexity belongs in the thinking process. If it remains in the final direction, teams may interpret the strategy differently or fail to act. If it is removed before the reasoning is complete, the result may be merely simplistic.

## Related distinctions

Simple strategy is not casual strategy. It compresses resolved complexity into a clear choice; it does not omit unresolved questions.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not use complexity as evidence of strategic quality.
- Do not simplify by hiding unresolved assumptions or trade-offs.
- Test whether the people executing the strategy can state the direction consistently.
