# Touchpoint priorities must be measured, not assumed

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when an organisation is prioritising customer-experience improvements from internal opinion or focusing only on the interactions it controls most visibly.

## Principle

Organisations can underestimate the width of the customer journey and misjudge which interactions matter most. Touchpoints differ in importance, and internal attention does not reliably reveal that difference. [^unknown-priorities]

Customer-described experiences should be measured for sentiment, variation, and prevalence before improvement priorities are chosen. [^measurement]

A weak or important touchpoint should then be investigated, changed, and remeasured rather than assumed to have improved. [^priority]

## Why it matters

Resources can be spent improving a visible but low-impact interaction while a more consequential part of the experience remains neglected.

## Related distinctions

Measurement identifies priority candidates; it does not explain the cause of poor performance. Qualitative investigation remains necessary before redesign.

## Invokes

- [Touchpoints](../frameworks/touchpoints.md) (Framework) — organises the complete experience and its measures.
- [Touch-Point Measurement and Prioritisation Process](../playbooks/touch-point-measurement-and-prioritisation-process.md) (Playbook) — gathers, analyses, prioritises, and remeasures touchpoint evidence.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not invent a touchpoint map from internal workshops alone.
- Do not prioritise only by average rating; also consider variation and prevalence.
- Do not assume a change worked without repeating the measurement.
