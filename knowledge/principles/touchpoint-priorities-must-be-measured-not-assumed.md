# Touchpoint priorities must be measured, not assumed

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when an organisation is prioritising customer-experience improvements from internal opinion or focusing only on the interactions it controls most visibly.

## Principle

Organisations can underestimate the width of the customer journey and misjudge which interactions matter most. Touchpoints differ in importance, and internal attention does not reliably reveal that difference.

Customer-described experiences should be measured for sentiment, variation, and prevalence before improvement priorities are chosen.

A weak or important touchpoint should then be investigated, changed, and remeasured rather than assumed to have improved.

## Why it matters

Resources can be spent improving a visible but low-impact interaction while a more consequential part of the experience remains neglected.

## Related distinctions

Measurement identifies priority candidates; it does not explain the cause of poor performance. Qualitative investigation remains necessary before redesign.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not invent a touchpoint map from internal workshops alone.
- Do not prioritise only by average rating; also consider variation and prevalence.
- Do not assume a change worked without repeating the measurement.
