# Positioning promises must be deliverable

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a proposed position describes a future aspiration that the organisation cannot yet deliver reliably.

## Principle

The capability to deliver a positioning promise must exist before the promise is made. Announcing an aspiration does not create the competence required to fulfil it.

Organisations are strong in some activities and weak in others. Positioning should focus on customer-valued claims the organisation can support through its actual competence, while unsupported activities are avoided, developed, or sourced elsewhere before becoming promises.

## Why it matters

A position that cannot be delivered creates a gap between strategy and customer experience.

## Related distinctions

An internal capability-development ambition can guide investment. It should not be presented to customers as a current promise until delivery is credible.

## Invokes

- [Three Cs positioning test](../playbooks/three-cs-positioning-test-consumer-competitor-company.md) (Playbook) — applies company capability as a required positioning test.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not substitute aspiration for present capability.
- Verify operational delivery across the relevant touchpoints.
- If capability must be built, separate that plan from the current market promise.
