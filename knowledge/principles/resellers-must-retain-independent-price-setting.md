# Resellers must retain independent price-setting

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a supplier wants a retailer or distributor to maintain a recommended resale price, reverse a discount, or avoid undercutting another channel.

## Principle

In the UK context described by the source, a reseller must remain able to set its resale price independently, subject to applicable special conditions. A supplier may recommend a price, but threats, supply consequences, or financial incentives can make that recommendation function as an unlawful minimum resale price.

The Fender case shows the risk: messages linking continued supply to restoring recommended prices were treated as unlawful resale price maintenance and resulted in a substantial fine.

## Why it matters

Attempts to resolve channel conflict by controlling resale prices can create severe legal, financial, and reputational exposure.

## Related distinctions

A recommended resale price is not the same as an enforceable minimum. The source explains the UK context and notes that terminology and special conditions can vary.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not threaten supply withdrawal or another penalty to force a reseller back to a recommended price.
- Do not use incentives that effectively create a minimum resale price.
- Do not treat this extracted Principle as jurisdiction-specific legal advice; verify current law and obtain qualified advice for the market concerned.
- Do not broaden the source's caution into a claim that every discussion of price with an external party is unlawful.
