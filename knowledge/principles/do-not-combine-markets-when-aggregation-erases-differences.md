# Do not combine markets when aggregation erases differences

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when deciding whether several countries, regions, or categories should share one segmentation map.

## Principle

A single combined segmentation is valuable only when the simplification preserves the distinctions needed for action. If combining country markets or materially different categories loses more than it gains, build separate segmentations. [^aggregation-choice]

## Why it matters

Fewer maps reduce complexity, but a neat global structure can become misleading when local market patterns differ. The decision is therefore a trade-off between manageability and lost meaning.

## Related distinctions

This is not a blanket requirement to create one map per country. It is a test of whether aggregation preserves the market structure relevant to decisions.

## Invokes

- [Segmentation Process](../playbooks/segmentation-process-top-level-5-step-sequence.md) (Playbook) — defines the market boundary before segmentation.
- [Standard Criteria for Segmentation](../playbooks/standard-criteria-for-segmentation-9-question-validation-checklist.md) (Playbook) — tests whether one map can validly capture the whole defined market.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not force different markets into one map merely to reduce the number of segmentations.
- Do not split markets automatically without testing whether one coherent map is possible.
- State the market boundary used by each map.
