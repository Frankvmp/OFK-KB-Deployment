# Pricing is the revenue-producing element of the marketing mix

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when pricing is being treated as a minor administrative choice or as interchangeable with the other tactical Ps.

## Principle

Pricing is distinctive within the marketing mix because it returns value from the market to the organisation when customers are willing to pay. The other Ps primarily require expenditure to create, communicate, and deliver the offer. [^return-flow]

Pricing is also the point at which the preceding marketing strategy meets the customer's decision to exchange money for the offer. [^moment-of-choice]

## Why it matters

Treating pricing as an afterthought disconnects tactical execution from the commercial return it is intended to produce. Pricing must be considered alongside product, communications, and distribution, while recognising its different economic role.

## Related distinctions

This Principle concerns pricing's role in the marketing mix. It does not reduce pricing to the numeric price: research, price setting, and price communication remain parts of the wider pricing process. [^moment-of-choice]

- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — places pricing within the coordinated tactical mix.
- [Value and profitability](../frameworks/value-and-profitability.md) (Framework) — explains how pricing returns part of the created value to the organisation.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not infer that revenue alone proves the price is profitable.
- Do not isolate pricing from the other Ps.
- Do not equate the pricing process with attaching a number to an offer.
