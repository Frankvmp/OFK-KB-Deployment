# Pricing power combines value perception with the ability to hold price

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when assessing the commercial strength of a brand, evaluating whether a higher price is supportable, or distinguishing brand equity from pricing execution.

## Principle

Pricing power is the ability to raise or maintain price without losing the business to competitors. It depends on both customer perceptions that justify the price and organisational capability to capture that value through pricing.

Strong product economics alone do not establish pricing power. Comparable products and production costs can produce very different profits when one organisation sustains a materially stronger selling price.

## Why it matters

This Principle separates value creation from value capture. A brand may have favourable perceptions but fail to convert them into price, while an attempted price increase without supporting value perceptions may lose demand.

## Related distinctions

Pricing power is not simply charging a premium. The relevant test is whether the price can be raised or held without losing business to a competitor.

## Invokes

- [Value and profitability](../frameworks/value-and-profitability.md) (Framework) — connects customer value, price, and the profit retained by the organisation.
- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — checks whether product, pricing, communications, and distribution collectively support the intended price.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not infer pricing power from a high list price alone.
- Do not treat brand perception as sufficient without pricing capability.
- Do not assume similar products or costs must produce similar profitability.
