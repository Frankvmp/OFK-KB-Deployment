# Targeting breadth must balance reach, cost, and message advantage

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when choosing between broad market reach and concentrating investment on one or more segments.

## Principle

Broad targeting is simpler and inclusive, and it can support penetration, but it reduces strategic selectivity, may blunt positioning, overlooks differences in customer value, and requires resources to reach a larger audience.

Focused targeting costs more per person because a smaller, more specific audience is harder to reach. The narrower target must therefore create enough additional customer value or message effectiveness to justify that premium.

## Why it matters

Neither maximum reach nor maximum precision is automatically superior. The right breadth depends on resources, differences in customer value, and whether greater relevance changes the expected result enough to offset lost reach.

## Related distinctions

This Principle governs the breadth decision. The targeting Playbook separately identifies the available philosophies and applies the segment-selection criteria.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not assume narrower targeting is automatically more efficient.
- Do not assume broad reach is affordable or equally valuable for every organisation.
- Make the expected benefit of narrower targeting explicit before paying its reach premium.
