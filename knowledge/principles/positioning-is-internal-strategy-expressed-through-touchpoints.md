# Positioning is internal strategy expressed through touchpoints

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when positioning is being treated as customer-facing copy, a strapline, or a document disconnected from tactical decisions.

## Principle

A positioning statement is an internal strategic reference. Customers do not experience the document directly; they infer the position through the brand's execution and the touchpoints they encounter. [^execution]

A strapline may express part of the position, but it is only one tactic. Positioning should guide communications, product, price, distribution, and the broader customer experience. [^not-strapline]

## Why it matters

A clear internal statement has no market effect unless the organisation translates it into consistent choices customers can experience.

## Related distinctions

Positioning is strategy. A strapline is one possible execution. Customer perception is the outcome produced across many executions.

## Invokes

- [Diagnosis → Strategy → Tactics](../frameworks/diagnosis-strategy-tactics.md) (Framework) — shows how strategic positioning reaches customers through tactical choices.
- [Strategy directs each tactical P](../frameworks/strategy-directs-each-tactical-p.md) (Framework) — tests product, price, communications, and distribution against the strategic position.
- [Touchpoints](../frameworks/touchpoints.md) (Framework) — identifies the encounters through which customers experience the position.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not expose the internal positioning statement as if it were finished customer copy.
- Do not reduce positioning to a strapline or advertising campaign.
- Check every major tactical choice and touchpoint for consistency with the position.
