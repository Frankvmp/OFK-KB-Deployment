# Direct-channel control does not guarantee scale or profitability

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when control, first-party data, subscription, or lower intermediary costs are being used as sufficient proof that a direct-only distribution model will scale profitably.

## Principle

Direct distribution can reduce intermediary costs and increase speed, simplicity, price control, customer data, and consumer proximity. It can also reduce retailer constraints and support subscription purchasing.

Those benefits do not establish profitable scale. In the module's D2C cases, many brands later added large retailers, conventional purchasing, and integrated communications because direct-only distribution did not provide enough scale, revenue, or profit.

## Why it matters

A channel can be strategically attractive at launch yet become constraining as the business grows. Treating direct distribution as an ideology can hide the need for complementary indirect reach.

## Related distinctions

This Principle does not say direct distribution is inferior. It says channel advantages and business outcomes must be tested separately, and the right mix can change with scale.

## Invokes

- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — keeps distribution coordinated with product, pricing, and communications as the route to market changes.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not infer profitability from the removal of intermediaries alone.
- Do not assume first-party data compensates for insufficient reach.
- Do not treat adding retail as proof that direct distribution had no value.
- Test channel economics and scale with current business evidence rather than generalising the cited D2C cases to every market.
