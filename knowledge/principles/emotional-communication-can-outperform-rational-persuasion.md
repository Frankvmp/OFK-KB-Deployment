# Emotional communication can outperform rational persuasion

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a campaign assumes that more product information or rational argument will necessarily produce the strongest response.

## Principle

Communications should deliberately consider what the target audience should feel, not only what it should think. In the cited analysis, overtly emotional campaigns produced profitable outcomes more often than rational campaigns or campaigns combining rational and emotional appeals. [^emotion]

## Why it matters

Defaulting to rational explanation can overlook an approach that the cited evidence associates with stronger campaign profitability.

## Related distinctions

The result is an observed average, not a guarantee that every emotional execution will outperform every rational one. The appropriate emotion and execution still depend on the target, position, and objective.

## Invokes

- [Target → position → objective](../frameworks/target-position-objective.md) (Framework) — supplies the strategic context for choosing the intended response.
- [Positioning and brand codes](../frameworks/positioning-and-brand-codes.md) (Framework) — connects the creative response to the brand association and identifiers it must express.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not use emotion without a strategic purpose.
- Do not treat a category average as a guaranteed campaign result.
- Do not assume that combining rational and emotional content is always optimal.
- Do not confuse emotional intensity with effective creative quality.
