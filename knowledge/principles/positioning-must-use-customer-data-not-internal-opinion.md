# Positioning must use customer data, not internal opinion

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when positioning attributes, competitors, or perceptual maps are being chosen from an internal workshop without customer evidence.

## Principle

Positioning should begin with evidence about what the target customer values most and which alternatives they actually consider. Internal views of what the organisation offers are not a substitute for those purchase drivers.

Perceptual analysis should likewise use real customer data. Simple maps based only on internal opinion can omit relevant attributes and misrepresent how close or distant brands appear in the market.

## Why it matters

Positioning is intended to alter or reinforce customer associations. Decisions made without customer evidence can optimise for meanings the market does not value or recognise.

## Related distinctions

Management judgement still chooses the position. Customer evidence supplies the market reality within which that judgement operates.

- [Market Research Method Sequence](../playbooks/market-research-method-sequence-secondary-qualitative-quantitative-mid-stage-focus-groups.md) (Playbook) — gathers evidence rather than inventing customer perceptions.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not infer customer priorities from company capabilities.
- Do not define competitors only from the organisation's category list.
- Label internally constructed maps as hypotheses until customer evidence tests them.
- Do not present a two-dimensional map as complete when more attributes matter.
