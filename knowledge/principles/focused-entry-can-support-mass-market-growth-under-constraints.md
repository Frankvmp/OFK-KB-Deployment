# Focused entry can support mass-market growth under constraints

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when the desired market is broader than the organisation can afford or operationally serve at the outset.

## Principle

A broad market ambition does not require immediate full-market reach. Resource limits can justify beginning within a geography, subcategory, or manageable segment.

A subcategory can support growth when a brand defines relevant must-have characteristics and becomes the exemplar of that space. Initial focus can then expand in stages as resources and capability grow.

## Why it matters

This separates long-term market intent from the practical scope of the first move. It allows concentration without claiming that the initial group is the brand's permanent boundary.

## Related distinctions

Focused entry is not necessarily traditional segment targeting. The initial group may be selected for affordability and manageability rather than because it requires a permanently different offer.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not present the initial focus as a permanent brand boundary unless the strategy requires it.
- Do not expand before the organisation can support broader reach.
- A subcategory claim needs clear defining characteristics; a label alone does not create one.
