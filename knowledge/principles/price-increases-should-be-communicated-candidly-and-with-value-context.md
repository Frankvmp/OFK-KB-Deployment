# Price increases should be communicated candidly and with value context

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when preparing or reviewing customer communication about a price increase.

## Principle

A price increase should be called an increase rather than hidden behind euphemistic language. Customers should receive adequate notice, a clear explanation of why the increase is needed and how the additional money will be used, and a reminder of the offer's continuing value.

Price-increase communication deserves the same customer focus and attention to detail as other brand-building work.

## Why it matters

Changing the amount without explaining and framing the change leaves customers with the price alone. Candour, rationale, notice, and value context help the organisation present the change as part of an ongoing customer relationship.

## Related distinctions

This Principle governs the content and posture of the communication. It does not determine the correct new price, which requires separate pricing research.

## Invokes

- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — coordinates the pricing change with the offer and its communications.
- [Value and profitability](../frameworks/value-and-profitability.md) (Framework) — keeps the rationale connected to customer value and organisational return.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not disguise an increase with vague wording.
- Do not announce the change without sufficient notice.
- Do not omit the reason or the use of the additional money.
- Do not repeat the new amount without restating the offer's value.
- Do not use communication quality to justify an unsupported price level.
