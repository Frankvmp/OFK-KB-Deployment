# Customer scope extends beyond the immediate buyer

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a business defines “the customer” as only the organisation that buys directly from it, or when research is planned around one intermediary without checking the end user and other relevant parties.

## Principle

Market understanding should include everyone relevant to the path from producer to ultimate consumer or user. The Module 1 example distinguishes the direct buyer, retailers, and end users rather than treating the direct buyer as the complete market.

## Why it matters

Listening only to the immediate customer can produce a partial or biased account of the market. An intermediary may accurately describe its own needs while still failing to represent what the end user wants.

The practical implication is not that every person must always receive identical research effort. It is that the agent should identify the relevant customer rings before deciding whose evidence is sufficient.

## Related distinctions

This Principle extends the scope of market understanding; it does not define a segmentation variable or replace the [Segmentation process](../playbooks/segmentation-process-top-level-5-step-sequence.md).

It also does not assume that the direct buyer and end user are always different. The source gives the customer-ring distinction through the Intel example, so the relationship must be checked for the specific market.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not treat the direct buyer's account as automatically representative of the whole market.
- Do not stop at “talk to everyone” as an unbounded instruction; identify which buyer, intermediary, user, or other party is relevant to the product and decision context.
