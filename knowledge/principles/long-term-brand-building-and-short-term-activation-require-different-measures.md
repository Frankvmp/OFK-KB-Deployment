# Long-term brand building and short-term activation require different measures

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when one return-on-investment measure is being applied to both future-demand activity and immediate-response activity.

## Principle

Long-term brand-building investment should be evaluated through changes in awareness, consideration, or brand perceptions. Short-term activation can be evaluated by comparing spend with the immediate response or uplift it produces.

## Why it matters

A measure should match the intended effect. Demanding immediate sales from activity designed to change future memory and perception can understate its contribution.

## Related distinctions

Different measures do not imply that long-term and short-term activity are disconnected. They serve complementary jobs but produce evidence on different time horizons.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not use immediate sales return as the sole test of long-term brand building.
- Do not use brand movement as the only test of a direct activation.
- Define the intended effect before selecting the measure.
- Do not convert contextual examples into universal performance thresholds.
