# Evidence-backed value supports holding the price line

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when retailers, managers, or customers are pressing for a discount despite evidence that the offer and strategy support the current price.

## Principle

When customer research, targeting, positioning, and product quality support the offer's value, the organisation should resist routine pressure to lower the price and retain the commercial benefit of the marketing work already completed.

Demand can be stimulated through non-price promotions that reinforce the brand and its value associations rather than reducing the amount charged.

## Why it matters

Automatic discounting gives away part of the value that research, strategy, product, and communications have created. Holding the line converts supported customer value into organisational value and profitability.

## Related distinctions

Holding price is not the same as always choosing a premium price. The price must first be supported by research and the wider marketing strategy. If those conditions are weak, the underlying problem should be corrected rather than defended.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not hold a price that lacks customer-value evidence.
- Do not defend a price when the product or strategy is materially wrong.
- Do not equate resisting discounts with selecting the highest possible price.
- Non-price promotions can be considered when they reinforce the brand and its value associations.
