# Communications investment and expected return must move together

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a communications budget is reduced while the associated revenue or growth forecast is left unchanged.

## Principle

Communications spending should be presented as an investment linked to an expected return. If management approves less investment than requested, the projected return must be recalculated for the lower amount rather than retained unchanged. [^linkage]

## Why it matters

Separating the return forecast from the resources required to produce it hides the commercial consequence of a budget decision and treats communications as an unrelated cost.

## Related distinctions

The principle does not guarantee that a proposed investment will produce its forecast return. It requires the plan to keep the investment assumption and return expectation logically connected.

## Invokes

- [Value and profitability](../frameworks/value-and-profitability.md) (Framework) — provides the wider commercial logic for evaluating value returned to the organisation.
- [Target → position → objective](../frameworks/target-position-objective.md) (Framework) — supplies the strategic objectives from which the investment case begins.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not preserve a return forecast after reducing its assumed investment.
- Do not treat a sales-ratio budget as evidence that the spend is strategically sufficient.
- Do not present an uncertain forecast as a guaranteed result.
- Rebuild both the amount and its allocation when using a zero base.
