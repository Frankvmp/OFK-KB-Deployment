# Segments must be coherent and large enough to serve

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when deciding whether a proposed group is a real market segment or merely a convenient label.

## Principle

A useful segment contains customers with relatively similar needs, motivations, or behaviour, while remaining meaningfully different from other groups in the market.

It must also be large enough to serve profitably. Segmentation therefore balances customer-specific relevance against the efficiencies of serving a group at scale.

## Why it matters

Without internal coherence, one offer or treatment will not fit the group. Without external difference, the group adds no useful distinction. Without sufficient scale, tailoring activity to it may be uneconomic.

## Related distinctions

This Principle defines the qualities of a segment. It does not specify how to construct the map; the [Meaningful Actionable Grid](../playbooks/meaningful-actionable-grid-10-stage-segmentation-construction-playbook.md) supplies that procedure.

Similarity is relative rather than absolute. Members need not be identical, but the group must be coherent enough for the intended marketing decision.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not treat every observable category as a segment.
- Do not create one-person segments merely to maximise similarity.
- Do not combine materially different customers solely to reduce the number of groups.
