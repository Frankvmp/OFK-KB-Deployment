# Choose positioning associations by fit, not hierarchy

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a team assumes emotional or purpose-led positioning is automatically superior to a category entry point, feature, or customer benefit.

## Principle

Higher levels of a benefit ladder are not inherently better. A category entry point, product feature, customer benefit, emotional association, or socio-cultural purpose can be the right positioning material depending on the target and market context.

A position may combine selected levels when they support the intended customer association. The choice should work backwards from what the target should think, rather than forcing every brand toward the top of the ladder.

## Why it matters

Treating the ladder as a quality ranking can replace a grounded position with a grander but less relevant claim.

## Related distinctions

The ladder is a vocabulary menu and analytical aid, not a mandatory sequence the brand must climb.

## Invokes

- [Positioning Statement Build Order](../playbooks/positioning-statement-build-order-to-what-versus-is.md) (Playbook) — records the chosen feature, benefit, entry point, or association in the “is” field.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not assume purpose is the best position because it is the highest rung.
- Do not combine levels unless they reinforce a coherent intended association.
- Choose from evidence about the target, alternatives, and company capability.
