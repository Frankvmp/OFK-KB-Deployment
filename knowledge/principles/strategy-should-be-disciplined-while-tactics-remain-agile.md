# Strategy should be disciplined while tactics remain agile

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when responding to executional change or when “agility” is being used to justify frequent changes to the strategy itself.

## Principle

Tactical execution should adapt when events cannot be predicted in advance. The governing strategy should remain disciplined and stable for its defined planning period.

Once research and earlier choices have been distilled into strategy, tactical teams should use that strategy as their guide rather than repeatedly reopening the entire diagnosis.

## Why it matters

Without tactical agility, execution becomes brittle. Without strategic discipline, each new event can pull activity in a different direction and dissolve the value of the earlier strategic work.

## Related distinctions

Discipline does not mean tactics are fixed. Agility does not mean target, position, and objectives should drift whenever an executional problem appears.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not confuse tactical adaptation with strategic revision.
- Revisit strategy when its assumptions or planning period materially change, not for every executional event.
- Do not use stability as a reason to ignore evidence that the strategy has become invalid.
