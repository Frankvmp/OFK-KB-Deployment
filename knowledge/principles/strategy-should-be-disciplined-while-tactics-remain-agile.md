# Strategy should be disciplined while tactics remain agile

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when responding to executional change or when “agility” is being used to justify frequent changes to the strategy itself.

## Principle

Tactical execution should adapt when events cannot be predicted in advance. The governing strategy should remain disciplined and stable for its defined planning period. [^agility]

Once research and earlier choices have been distilled into strategy, tactical teams should use that strategy as their guide rather than repeatedly reopening the entire diagnosis. [^distillation]

## Why it matters

Without tactical agility, execution becomes brittle. Without strategic discipline, each new event can pull activity in a different direction and dissolve the value of the earlier strategic work.

## Related distinctions

Discipline does not mean tactics are fixed. Agility does not mean target, position, and objectives should drift whenever an executional problem appears.

## Invokes

- [Diagnosis → Strategy → Tactics](../frameworks/diagnosis-strategy-tactics.md) (Framework) — distinguishes stable strategic direction from adaptable tactical execution.
- [Strategy directs each tactical P](../frameworks/strategy-directs-each-tactical-p.md) (Framework) — applies the settled strategy to product, pricing, communications, and distribution choices.
- [Target → position → objective](../frameworks/target-position-objective.md) (Framework) — provides the stable strategic reference points.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not confuse tactical adaptation with strategic revision.
- Revisit strategy when its assumptions or planning period materially change, not for every executional event.
- Do not use stability as a reason to ignore evidence that the strategy has become invalid.
