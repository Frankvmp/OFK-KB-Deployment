# Existing-customer evidence can refine positioning

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when positioning an existing offer with customers and sales history, rather than creating a position for an unlaunched product.

## Principle

Positioning for an existing offer can evolve as the organisation learns which alternatives customers considered and which strengths made the offer attractive. Deliberate testing and refinement are required because an effective position rarely appears automatically. [^evolutionary]

Customers who already prefer the offer can provide evidence about why it wins. Their repeated explanations can reveal an important association that the original position omitted. [^loyalists]

## Why it matters

Existing customers provide observed evidence of a successful match between offer and market. Ignoring them can leave positioning based only on pre-launch assumptions.

## Related distinctions

Loyal customers are an evidence source, not the sole target by default. Their insights must still be tested for relevance to the intended market.

## Invokes

- [Market Research Method Sequence](../playbooks/market-research-method-sequence-secondary-qualitative-quantitative-mid-stage-focus-groups.md) (Playbook) — structures the collection and validation of customer evidence.
- [Three Cs positioning test](../playbooks/three-cs-positioning-test-consumer-competitor-company.md) (Playbook) — retests the refined position against customer, competitor, and company.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not treat one enthusiastic customer as representative evidence.
- Do not copy customer language without testing its strategic relevance.
- Do not assume the original position must remain unchanged when evidence reveals a stronger association.
