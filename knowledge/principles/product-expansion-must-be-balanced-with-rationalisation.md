# Product expansion must be balanced with rationalisation

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a portfolio is expanding through new products, extensions, acquisitions, channel variants, or co-brands without an equivalent review of what should be removed.

## Principle

Repeated diversification can produce too many products, especially when organisations reward launches and revenue while treating removal as failure. [^proliferation]

Rationalisation can reduce cost and concentrate effort on stronger products, even when it causes a temporary reduction in revenue. The released focus and profitability can create capacity for later improvement and renewed growth. [^rationalisation]

A practical governance expression is to pair proposed new SKUs with explicit candidates for removal. [^replacement-rule]

## Why it matters

Portfolio breadth consumes manufacturing, distribution, management, and marketing resources. Without removal, weak products dilute attention and make the whole portfolio harder to improve.

## Related distinctions

Rationalisation is not opposition to innovation. Expansion and contraction form a continuing portfolio cycle, with evidence determining which products deserve resources.

## Invokes

- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — contains the product rationalisation path and its relationship to later diversification.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not evaluate products by revenue alone; examine profit contribution and resource burden.
- Do not hide the possibility of a temporary revenue decline.
- Do not remove products without product-level evidence and transition planning.
