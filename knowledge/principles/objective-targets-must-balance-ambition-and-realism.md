# Objective targets must balance ambition and realism

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when selecting the future value in a measurable objective, especially when the proposed target is arbitrary or based on aspiration alone.

## Principle

An objective should be ambitious enough for the expected improvement to matter and realistic enough to support credible plans and budgets. Resolving that tension is an evidence-informed judgement rather than a fixed formula. [^tension]

Calibration should draw on past performance, comparable segments or markets, competitive benchmarks, and relevant delivery experience. The attainable rate of improvement can also differ by funnel stage. [^evidence]

## Why it matters

An easy objective may not justify the investment, while an implausible objective distorts budgets and creates a false basis for accountability. Evidence makes the stretch explicit without pretending the future is precisely knowable.

## Related distinctions

Realistic does not mean conservative, and ambitious does not mean unconstrained. The target should be both consequential and defensible.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not select a target value without a current benchmark.
- Do not copy an improvement rate from a different funnel stage without testing comparability.
- Do not present judgement-based projections as certain forecasts.
