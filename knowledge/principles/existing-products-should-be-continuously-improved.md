# Existing products should be continuously improved

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when product work is framed only as new-product development or when an established offer is treated as finished.

## Principle

Marketing's product responsibility continues after launch. Existing customers provide both revenue and evidence that can be used to improve and augment an in-market offer. [^existing-product]

Improvement need not depend on breakthrough innovation. Repeated, evidence-led changes to an existing product can be a more accessible and valuable contribution than creating a product from scratch. [^continuous-improvement]

Touchpoints, Net Promoter Score, and Jobs to Be Done are presented as three routes for finding existing-product improvements. [^three-methods]

## Why it matters

Most marketers encounter products that already exist. Treating those products as fixed discards customer feedback and narrows the product role to rare development projects.

## Related distinctions

Continuous improvement is not uncontrolled feature accumulation. Changes should address evidenced customer jobs or experience problems and remain aligned with the strategy.

## Invokes

- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — places existing-product improvement within the product decision area.
- [Touchpoints](../frameworks/touchpoints.md) (Framework) — identifies experiences that can reveal improvement opportunities.
- [Touch-Point Measurement and Prioritisation Process](../playbooks/touch-point-measurement-and-prioritisation-process.md) (Playbook) — selects and remeasures a priority experience.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not reserve product input only for new-product launches.
- Do not assume an in-market product is optimal because it is currently successful.
- Do not add changes without customer evidence or strategic fit.
