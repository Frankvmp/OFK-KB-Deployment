# Early distribution should establish the brand before maximising availability

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a new brand is choosing between an early channel that expresses its positioning and a high-volume channel that offers immediate reach.

## Principle

At the beginning of a brand's distribution, channel choice should primarily establish the intended brand experience and positioning. The initial setting can influence perceived value, price expectations, and which channels become viable later.

Broad physical availability remains important, but it need not be activated all at once. Distribution can expand gradually from brand-building channels toward channels that provide greater scale, profit, and revenue once the brand has been established.

## Why it matters

A scale-first launch can create a low-price or mass-market signal that is difficult to reverse. It can also make premium channels less willing to carry the brand.

## Related distinctions

This Principle governs sequencing, not permanent exclusivity. It does not say that every brand must launch in restaurants or avoid large retailers indefinitely.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not assume the highest-volume launch channel is automatically the best starting point.
- Do not copy Fiji Water's specific on-premise sequence without checking the brand's strategy and market.
- Do not interpret gradual rollout as a reason to suppress viable availability after the brand-building purpose has been served.
- Separate authorised channel choices from grey-market leakage that the brand did not choose.
