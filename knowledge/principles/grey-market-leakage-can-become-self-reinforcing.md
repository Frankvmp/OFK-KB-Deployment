# Grey-market leakage can become self-reinforcing

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when genuine products appear through unintended channels, territories, or prices outside the brand's agreed route to market.

## Principle

Grey-market products are genuine and legally sold, but they move outside the intended distribution agreement through parallel sources. Excess stock, wholesalers, or corporate affiliates can introduce this leakage. Once it starts, wider availability at unintended prices can weaken channel discipline and encourage further leakage. [^sources-cycle]

Grey markets may not disappear completely. They require continuing distribution discipline and management to limit their spread and effects. [^manage]

## Why it matters

Parallel distribution can disrupt authorised partner economics, price architecture, positioning, and launch sequencing even though the goods are not counterfeit.

## Related distinctions

A grey market concerns genuine goods sold through unintended but legal routes. A black market involves stolen, counterfeit, or otherwise illegal goods.

## Invokes

- [Value and profitability](../frameworks/value-and-profitability.md) (Framework) — supports assessment of how parallel routes redistribute margin and affect the brand's retained value.
- [Distribution Cadence (brand-first, phased channel rollout)](../playbooks/distribution-cadence-brand-first-phased-channel-rollout.md) (Playbook) — shows the intended channel sequence that grey-market leakage can disrupt.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not classify discounted goods as counterfeit without evidence.
- Do not assume every unauthorised route is illegal.
- Do not claim that grey markets can be eliminated completely.
- Do not infer the source of leakage without tracing real inventory and channel evidence.
