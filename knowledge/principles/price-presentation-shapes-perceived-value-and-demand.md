# Price presentation shapes perceived value and demand

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when designing a price display, offer comparison, anchor, package, or choice set.

## Principle

The way a price is presented can change perceived value and demand independently of the underlying product. In the cited wine experiment, different stated prices changed both reported experience and measured pleasure response for the same wine.

Customers often assess an offer relative to immediately available reference points. Anchors, premium comparisons, and decoy options can therefore change how later prices and choices are evaluated.

## Why it matters

Price communication is not a cosmetic step after the amount has been selected. It is part of pricing because the frame helps customers interpret whether the offer represents value.

## Related distinctions

Framing does not change the nominal amount being charged. It changes the context through which that amount and the offer are interpreted. This Principle governs the effect; specific devices such as charm pricing, anchoring, and decoy options are tactical applications.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not treat presentation as a substitute for a credible offer.
- Do not assume customers evaluate every price through complete information and calculation.
- Do not confuse a framing effect with evidence that any framing device will work in every context.
- Do not misrepresent the actual price or terms.
