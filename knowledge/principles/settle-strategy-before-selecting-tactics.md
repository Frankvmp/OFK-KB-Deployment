# Settle strategy before selecting tactics

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when product, price, communications, or distribution ideas are being chosen before the strategic choices are complete.

## Principle

Strategy must direct tactics. Tactical capabilities or ideas should not determine the strategy from the bottom up. [^strategy-directs]

Complete and test the strategic direction before selecting the tactical actions that will express it. Jumping into execution early leaves activity without a coherent basis. [^no-tactical-jump]

## Why it matters

A tactic can be individually attractive while pulling in a different direction from the target, position, or objective. Sequencing keeps execution aligned.

## Related distinctions

This Principle does not delay all operational work until every uncertainty disappears. It requires the governing strategic choices to be clear before tactics are selected or approved.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not infer strategy from a preferred tactic.
- Do not treat tactical urgency as permission to skip target, position, or objective choices.
- Reject tactics that conflict with the settled strategy, even when they are attractive in isolation.
