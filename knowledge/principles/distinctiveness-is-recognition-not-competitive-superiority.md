# Distinctiveness is recognition, not competitive superiority

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when recognisability is being confused with a claim that a brand is better than its alternatives.

## Principle

Distinctiveness concerns whether people can easily identify something as belonging to a particular brand. It asks whether the brand looks like itself and comes to mind, independently of a competitive comparison. [^distinction] [^recognition]

Differentiation is a separate question about how the brand is perceived relative to alternatives. A brand can therefore be recognisable without being preferred, or relatively preferred without being easy to recognise. [^distinction]

## Why it matters

Separating the two jobs prevents a distinctive asset from being mistaken for a customer benefit or competitive advantage.

## Related distinctions

A code identifies the brand. A position supplies the intended associations that may create relative preference.

## Invokes

- [Positioning and brand codes](../frameworks/positioning-and-brand-codes.md) (Framework) — holds distinctiveness and differentiation together while preserving their different roles.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not evaluate a code only by whether it communicates a benefit.
- Do not assume easy recognition establishes meaningful superiority.
- Keep recognisability and relative preference as separate tests.
