# Clients should provide strategy and preserve agency creative autonomy

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a client brief mixes strategic direction with proposed advertisements, creative concepts, or agency tactics.

## Principle

A client should complete the strategy and brief the target, position, objectives, budget, and other constraints. The client should stop short of supplying creative approaches or tactical ideas, because developing the creative response is the agency's role. [^autonomy]

## Why it matters

A clear division of responsibility gives the agency a stable strategic platform without narrowing the creative work to the client's undeveloped ideas.

## Related distinctions

Creative autonomy does not mean strategic autonomy. The agency interprets the brief creatively; the client remains responsible for the strategy and for integrating agencies and channels around it.

## Invokes

- [Strategy directs each tactical P](../frameworks/strategy-directs-each-tactical-p.md) (Framework) — provides the strategic test for communications choices.
- [Settle the Strategy Before Execution](../playbooks/settle-the-strategy-before-execution.md) (Playbook) — completes and challenges the strategy before briefing and execution.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not brief an agency before target, position, and objectives are clear.
- Do not turn the client brief into a prescribed creative execution.
- Do not treat creative freedom as permission to ignore the strategy.
- Do not delegate cross-agency integration back to competing agencies.
