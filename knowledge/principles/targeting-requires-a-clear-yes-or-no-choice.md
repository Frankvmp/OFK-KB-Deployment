# Targeting requires a clear yes-or-no choice

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when every segment appears on a numbered priority list and no segment is clearly outside active investment.

## Principle

Targeting requires a clear decision about whether each candidate segment will receive focused investment. Priority rankings can conceal an unwillingness to sacrifice by retaining every segment as a target.

## Why it matters

If every segment remains a target at some priority, resources can still spread across the whole market. A clear yes-or-no decision protects strategic focus.

## Related distinctions

This Principle applies to target selection, not to the later allocation of different budgets among the selected targets. Selected targets may still receive unequal investment.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not use priority labels as a substitute for rejecting segments.
- Do not infer that every selected target must receive equal resources.
- Document the non-targets as clearly as the targets.
