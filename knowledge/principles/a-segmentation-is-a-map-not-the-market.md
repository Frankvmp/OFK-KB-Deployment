# A segmentation is a map, not the market

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when estimating segment size or value, interpreting calculated outputs, or deciding whether to rebuild an established segmentation.

## Principle

A segmentation is an abstraction of the market. Its populations and values often project from samples and estimates, so they should be broadly accurate without being presented as exact. Directional checks should catch major magnitude errors.

As the market changes, update populations, values, and shares. Preserve the established structure unless evidence shows that the segmentation itself no longer works.

## Why it matters

False precision hides uncertainty, while constant reinvention destroys organisational familiarity and comparability. The useful standard is transparent approximation, adequate validation, and disciplined maintenance.

## Related distinctions

Approximate does not mean arbitrary. Estimates still require evidence, explicit assumptions, and checks against other observations.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not disguise estimates as exact measurements.
- Do not accept large magnitude inconsistencies merely because estimates are approximate.
- Do not rebuild segment names and structure annually when updated numbers are sufficient.
