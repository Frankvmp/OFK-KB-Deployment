# Consolidated retail power must be managed, not assumed controllable

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a brand is searching for a decisive tactic to make a large retailer change its independently chosen price or presentation.

## Principle

Large, consolidated retailers can hold substantial power. The Dom Pérignon case presents no practical manoeuvre that restores supplier power over a consolidated retailer's chosen resale price; several proposed responses were described as illegal. The practical task is to manage that reality rather than pretend it can be removed. [^power-reality]

Management can include reducing avoidable presentation damage and using the revenue and profit from the channel to strengthen brand investment elsewhere. [^offset]

## Why it matters

A false assumption of control can lead to commercially unrealistic or unlawful responses. Accepting the power constraint shifts attention toward actions the brand can actually take.

## Related distinctions

Accepting a power constraint is not passive approval of every retailer action. It means distinguishing controllable brand decisions from outcomes that belong to an independent reseller.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not promise a tactic that will restore supplier control over an independent retailer.
- Do not recommend threats or supply pressure as a pricing remedy.
- Do not assume withdrawal is commercially or legally available without evidence.
- Do not generalise one retailer relationship without assessing the actual power, economics, contracts, and law.
