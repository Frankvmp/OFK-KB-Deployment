# Research methods are trade-offs, not a ranking

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when choosing a research method, comparing proposals, or interpreting evidence from one method as if it were complete. It helps an agent ask what each method can and cannot establish before recommending it.

## Principle

Research methods are not a universal best-to-worst ranking. Each method offers useful capabilities and carries limitations, so selection must follow the evidence need rather than a fixed hierarchy.

Secondary data offers speed and low cost; surveys offer quantitative breadth; focus groups offer rapid qualitative exploration; ethnography offers contextual depth; and conjoint analysis derives choice drivers from trade-offs. The source presents these as different forms of illumination, not interchangeable substitutes.

## Why it matters

A method can be executed correctly and still be wrong for the question. Treating one method as universally superior hides its boundaries and can produce precise but incomplete evidence.

The practical response is to combine methods where their strengths complement one another, while keeping each method's evidential limits visible.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not rank methods without reference to the research question.
- Do not treat evidence from one method as if it provides every kind of insight.
- Do not infer the missing research-architecture process beyond the source cutoff at 00:59:31.
