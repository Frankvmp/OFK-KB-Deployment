# Communications should be measured at campaign level and used to improve

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when communications is judged only by overall company sales or profit, or when measurement is used only to defend past activity.

## Principle

A campaign should be assessed against its own performance and stated objectives, not only against overall company results. Product and pricing problems can affect company sales or profit independently of communications quality. [^campaign-level]

Measurement should also support learning. Evidence about creative and media performance should be used to optimise the campaign and improve later execution. [^learning]

## Why it matters

A company-level result can misattribute success or failure. Campaign-level evidence creates a clearer basis for evaluation and for changing the parts of communications that can actually be improved.

## Related distinctions

Campaign-level measurement does not mean ignoring commercial outcomes. It separates the campaign's contribution from other business factors before drawing conclusions.

## Invokes

- [Brand tracking](../frameworks/brand-tracking.md) (Framework) — evaluates long-term campaign outcomes and distinguishes relevant measurement methods.
- [Target → position → objective](../frameworks/target-position-objective.md) (Framework) — provides the stated objective against which campaign performance is assessed.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not use overall sales or profit as a clean proxy for communications quality.
- Do not claim causal contribution from simple first- or last-touch attribution.
- Match the method to the question and channel scope.
- Use findings to adjust decisions rather than only to report success.
