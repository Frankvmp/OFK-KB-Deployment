# Funnel performance needs conversion and competitive context

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a funnel stage appears weak or strong based only on its percentage of the total market.

## Principle

A funnel stage should be interpreted through its conversion from the preceding stage. An isolated population percentage can make a well-performing stage appear weak because it ignores how many eligible customers reached it.

Competitive or category comparison adds a second necessary context. A low absolute result may outperform alternatives, while a seemingly healthy result may contain the largest relative weakness.

## Why it matters

Objectives and tactics chosen from isolated percentages can attack the wrong problem. Conversion and competitive context help locate the stage where improvement is both needed and plausible.

## Related distinctions

Conversion and comparison identify where performance differs. They do not explain the reason for the gap; bridge analysis or further research is needed for that diagnosis.

## Invokes

- [Funnel](../frameworks/funnel.md) (Framework) — calculates conversion, adds comparison, and supports bridge analysis.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not rank stages by total-market percentage alone.
- Do not treat a competitor average as a universal performance threshold.
- Do not infer the cause of underperformance from the comparison itself.
