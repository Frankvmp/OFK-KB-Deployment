# Map the whole market before choosing targets

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a segmentation is being shaped around preferred customers, or when targeting choices are entering the diagnostic stage too early.

## Principle

Segmentation describes the market; targeting makes company-specific choices. The segmentation must therefore cover every customer inside the defined market, including groups the organisation may later reject. [^diagnosis-boundary] [^whole-market-check]

A completed map may reveal attractive and unattractive opportunities, but the decision to pursue them belongs to the targeting stage. [^strategy-handoff]

## Why it matters

Building only around desired customers hides alternatives and turns a market diagnosis into a justification for a choice already made. A whole-market map makes both selection and deliberate exclusion possible.

## Related distinctions

The [Segmentation Process](../playbooks/segmentation-process-top-level-5-step-sequence.md) produces the map. Targeting consumes that map and chooses where the organisation will compete. The two activities are connected, but they are not interchangeable.

- [Segmentation Process](../playbooks/segmentation-process-top-level-5-step-sequence.md) (Playbook) — builds and validates the whole-market map.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not exclude a segment merely because it is unlikely to be targeted.
- Do not label segments by target priority during segmentation.
- Do not interpret an apparent opportunity as a targeting decision until the map is complete.
