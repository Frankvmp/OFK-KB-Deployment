# Customers buy solutions to jobs, not products as defined internally

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when improving a product, defining its competition, or communicating it from the organisation's category and feature language.

## Principle

Customers seek a solution to an underlying need or challenge, which may differ from what the organisation believes it sells. Product work should therefore investigate the job the customer is trying to accomplish. [^solution]

Starting with the job can reveal alternatives outside the assumed category, clarify how to communicate the offer, and identify improvements that help the product perform the job better. [^job]

## Why it matters

Internal product definitions create category blinders. A job-led view reframes competition and development around the outcome the customer is actually seeking.

## Related distinctions

The job is not the customer's demographic identity. Customer characteristics may still matter, but the immediate product question concerns the progress or outcome being sought. [^job]

## Invokes

- [Market orientation](../frameworks/market-orientation.md) (Framework) — shifts interpretation from the producer's definition to the customer's perspective.
- [Four Ps](../frameworks/four-ps.md) (Framework) — applies the job insight to product improvement and communication.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not define the job from product features alone.
- Do not limit alternatives to the organisation's existing category.
- Do not treat an appealing job statement as evidence without customer observation or inquiry.
