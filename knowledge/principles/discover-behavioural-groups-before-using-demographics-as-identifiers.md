# Discover behavioural groups before using demographics as identifiers

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a segmentation begins with age, gender, income, or another easy identifier and then assumes what those people need.

## Principle

Discover groups from relevant attitudes, choices, and category behaviour before asking whether demographic variables help identify them. [^research-inputs]

This reverses demographic-first inference: it establishes a behaviourally coherent group, then tests whether age, gender, income, or other identifiers occur more often within it. [^reverse-inference]

## Why it matters

Demographics can make a group easy to locate without explaining why it behaves differently. Behaviour-first grouping keeps the segment grounded in evidence about the market decision while retaining demographics for recognition and access.

## Related distinctions

Demographics remain useful identifiers. The Principle rejects treating them as automatic evidence of needs, motivations, or behaviour.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not infer needs from demographic membership alone.
- Do not claim every member shares the group's average demographic profile.
- Do not omit demographic data when it can help identify an evidence-based behavioural group.
