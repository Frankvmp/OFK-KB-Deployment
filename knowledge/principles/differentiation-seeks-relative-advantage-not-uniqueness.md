# Differentiation seeks relative advantage, not uniqueness

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when positioning work is stalled by a search for an attribute no competitor can claim.

## Principle

Differentiation does not require a unique product quality or exclusive ownership of an association. A brand can be different by being perceived as relatively stronger than alternatives on attributes that matter to the target customer.

Relative strength requires focus on a small number of selected associations and sustained delivery across touchpoints. Trying to stand for too many things prevents the concentration needed to become stronger on any of them.

## Why it matters

The search for uniqueness can reject credible sources of preference merely because competitors also possess them.

## Related distinctions

Sharing an attribute with competitors does not make brands equivalent. The relevant question is whether the focal brand is perceived as stronger on that attribute in the target market.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not require absolute uniqueness.
- Do not select attributes that do not matter to the target customer.
- Do not claim relative advantage without comparing real alternatives.
- Concentrate on a small number of associations.
