# Price should follow customer perceived value, not cost-plus arithmetic

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a proposed price is being calculated mainly from production cost and a standard markup.

## Principle

Costs establish a floor that the price must normally exceed, but they should not determine the price through a routine markup. Cost-plus pricing excludes customer value, assumes a uniform market, and may omit material business costs. [^cost-plus]

The practical price-setting guide is the customer's perceived value. The organisation should research that value and seek a price close to it, while avoiding both overpricing and underpricing. [^perceived-value]

## Why it matters

Cost-plus arithmetic answers what the organisation spent, not what the market is prepared to exchange for the offer. Starting with customer perceived value keeps pricing market-oriented and makes room for differences between segments.

## Related distinctions

True economic value describes the offer's theoretical economic benefit. The source treats customer perceived value as the more practical pricing guide, while noting that marketing can increase perceived value toward the economic value. [^perceived-value]

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not ignore the cost floor.
- Do not treat true economic value as automatically perceived by customers.
- Do not infer perceived value from internal opinion.
- Do not assume one price suits every segment.
