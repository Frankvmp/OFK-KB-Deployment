# NPS is a contextual learning measure, not a growth predictor

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when interpreting Net Promoter Score, comparing scores, or deciding what the measure can legitimately support.

## Principle

An NPS result has no universal good or bad meaning. It should be interpreted against relevant category and country norms, the brand's history, and competitor results.

NPS should not be treated as the only customer measure or as a dependable predictor of future growth. Its value lies in comparative tracking and in the customer explanations gathered after the score.

Promoter, passive, and detractor comments can reveal what should be retained, what is weak, and what may need improvement.

## Why it matters

An absolute score can produce false confidence or alarm. Context and explanation turn the number into evidence for learning rather than an unsupported commercial forecast.

## Related distinctions

The quantitative score benchmarks sentiment. The qualitative follow-up supplies reasons. Neither alone establishes that a particular change will cause growth.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not compare scores across categories or countries without context.
- Do not claim NPS alone predicts growth.
- Do not discard the qualitative follow-up after calculating the score.
