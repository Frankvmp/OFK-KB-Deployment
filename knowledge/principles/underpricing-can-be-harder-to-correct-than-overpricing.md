# Underpricing can be harder to correct than overpricing

*(New here? See AGENTS.md at this repository's root for how to use it.)*

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a team proposes a deliberately low launch price, assumes a price can easily be raised later, or treats overpricing as the only material pricing risk.

## Principle

Overpricing can suppress demand and may be corrected by lowering the price. Underpricing can be more difficult to repair because it forgoes revenue and profit while establishing the offer at a low perceived-value position in the market.

Once a low price is established, raising it can be difficult. The source also identifies a managerial tendency to favour volume and set prices below the value the market might support.

## Why it matters

A low starting price is not a neutral trial. It can shape customer expectations and constrain later attempts to capture value.

## Related distinctions

This Principle does not say that higher prices are always better. It says that both errors matter and that the consequences of an initially low price may be less reversible.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not use this Principle to justify a price unsupported by customer value.
- Do not assume a low launch price can be raised without changing expectations.
- Do not treat volume growth as proof that an underpriced offer is commercially healthy.
