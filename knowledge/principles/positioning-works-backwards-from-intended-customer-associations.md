# Positioning works backwards from intended customer associations

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when positioning activity is centred on completing a brand document, choosing fashionable terminology, or accumulating statements.

## Principle

Positioning is a means to an end: the intended associations that should come to a target customer's mind when they think about the brand. The work should start with that desired mental outcome and proceed backwards to a concise articulation. [^end]

The organisation may call that articulation a belief, promise, purpose, values, essence, or another label. The label is secondary; the strategic task is to choose one compact way to express the intended associations. [^articulation]

## Why it matters

Treating the document as the outcome encourages extra layers and words. Working backwards keeps attention on the customer effect the strategy is meant to create.

## Related distinctions

The internal articulation describes the intended association. It is not itself the customer's actual perception or the external execution.

## Invokes

- [Three Cs positioning test](../playbooks/three-cs-positioning-test-consumer-competitor-company.md) (Playbook) — tests the intended association against customer, competitor, and company evidence.
- [Positioning Statement Build Order](../playbooks/positioning-statement-build-order-to-what-versus-is.md) (Playbook) — expresses the selected association in a concrete record.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not treat completion of a brand document as proof of customer impact.
- Do not stack multiple positioning formats without a clear need.
- Keep the intended customer association explicit and concise.
