# Pricing decisions require customer evidence, not internal intuition

## When to use this

Reach for this Principle when a price is being justified by inherited rules, competitor prices, a standard markup, or untested managerial judgement.

## Principle

Pricing decisions should be informed by customer evidence about perceived value, willingness to pay, and demand. Combining cost data, competitor prices, and inherited anecdotes without customer research is an inferior basis for price setting. [^weak-inputs]

No single pricing-research method is universally best. Van Westendorp, conjoint analysis, experimentation, and dynamic pricing differ in cost, accuracy, suitability, and the behaviour they can reveal. The method should fit the market and decision. [^method-fit]

## Why it matters

Unaided judgement can reproduce organisational assumptions and systematic underpricing. Customer evidence gives the pricing process an external reference rather than letting the organisation price from its own perspective.

## Related distinctions

Research informs the pricing decision; it does not remove managerial judgement. A method can also be well executed but poorly matched to the product or market.

## Invokes

- [Research informs understanding, not strategy](../frameworks/research-informs-understanding-not-strategy.md) (Framework) — keeps research as evidence for a pricing choice rather than an automatic answer.
- [Conjoint Analysis Procedure](../playbooks/conjoint-analysis-procedure.md) (Playbook) — derives attribute and price value from repeated trade-offs.
- [Seven-Step Route Out of Discounting](../playbooks/seven-step-route-out-of-discounting.md) (Playbook) — requires research to establish the destination price before reducing promotional dependence.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Do not replace customer evidence with competitor imitation.
- Do not treat a stated willingness-to-pay answer as complete behavioural proof.
- Do not apply a research method without checking its suitability.
- Do not present research as eliminating uncertainty or judgement.
